How it works

  1. Paste your script. One line per cut — a cut is one stretch of filming, from starting the camera to stopping it. Press Enter to start a new one.
  2. Put the phone behind your camera and press start. A countdown gives the microphone time to wake up, so the first word is never the one that gets lost.
  3. Talk. The words fill in as you say them. At the end of a cut the prompter stops and waits — it will not run ahead of you while you reset.
  4. Say what you want next. “Take that again” goes back to the top of the cut. “That’s the one” marks the take you want to keep. “Move on” goes to the next cut.
  5. Take the log with you. Every attempt at every cut, with its clock time and the one you marked, ready to copy into whatever you edit in.

What makes it different

It waits at every cut

Most prompters scroll at a speed you set and keep going whether or not you are with them. This one stops where your take stops.

A restart is a sentence, not a button

Your hands are on the camera or out of shot. Say “take that again” and the words go back to the top of the cut.

It forgives a paraphrase

Skip a word, swap one, say it your own way — the words keep up. It is matching what you mean, not waiting for exactly what you wrote.

It keeps a take log

Which cut, which attempt, how long, and the one you said to keep. It never scores a take: it cannot see your eyeline or hear the noise outside.

What you can say

Spoken while you are reading, hands free. Inside a cut, say “prompter” first — that is this tool’s own name, short — so your own script is never mistaken for an instruction. Between cuts, the plain words are enough. You can change any of these phrases to your own.

“take that again”back to the top of this cut, for another attempt
“that’s the one”marks the attempt you just did as the keeper
“move on”on to the next cut
“back up”to the previous cut
“from the top”back to the first word of the script
“that’s a wrap”ends the session and shows the take log

Which browsers can listen

The listening is the browser’s own, not ours, so what it can do depends on where you open it. Anywhere it cannot listen, the prompter still prompts — the words move at a steady pace you set instead.

Where your words go

Your script never leaves your browser. There is no account, no server of ours, and nothing to sign up for — what you paste is kept on your own device and is still there when you come back.

The listening is done by your browser’s own speech recognition. In Chrome that normally means the sound of your voice goes to Google to be turned into words, and in Safari to Apple; the page asks Chrome to do it on your device instead wherever it can, and tells you which one you got. We never receive your voice, and nothing is recorded.

Questions

Is it free?

Yes, and there is nothing to sign up for. Open the page and paste a script.

Do I need to install anything?

No. It runs in the browser. On a phone you can add it to your home screen, and it then opens full-screen like an app, with no address bar taking up reading room.

What if I do not say it exactly as written?

It keeps up. The matching is on what you mean rather than on the exact words, so a skipped word or a rephrased sentence does not lose your place. If it does lose you, it says so plainly rather than guessing.

Does it record me?

No. It listens to place you in the script and keeps a written log of your takes. No audio and no video is recorded or kept.

Can I use it with a teleprompter rig?

Yes. There is a mirror setting for rigs with glass in front of the lens, which reverses the words so they read correctly in the glass.

What is the take log for?

So you are not scrubbing through footage guessing which attempt was the good one. Every attempt is listed with its clock time and the words you actually said, and the one you marked out loud is marked in the log.
